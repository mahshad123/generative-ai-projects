Q1. What was the objective of the Apollo 11 mission?
Expected response:
    The Apollo 11 mission's objective was to perform the first crewed lunar
    landing and return the crew safely to Earth. It aimed to land astronauts
    Neil Armstrong and Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin on the Moon (with Michael Collins in
    the Command Module) in July 1969.


Q2. What emergency occurred during Apollo 13 and how did the crew respond?
Expected response:
    An oxygen tank in the Service Module exploded on April 13, 1970, while the
    spacecraft was en route to the Moon, causing a critical loss of power,
    oxygen, and water. The Moon landing was aborted. The crew used the Lunar
    Module as a "lifeboat," conserved power and water, and worked with Mission
    Control to return safely, splashing down on April 17, 1970.


Q3. What happened during the Challenger launch?
Expected response:
    The Space Shuttle Challenger (mission STS-51L) broke apart about 73 seconds
    after liftoff on January 28, 1986, resulting in the loss of all seven crew
    members. The answer should be grounded in the retrieved mission-audio
    transcripts; if the retrieved context does not contain a specific detail,
    the system should say so rather than guess.


Q4. Who were the crew members of Apollo 13?
Expected response:
    The Apollo 13 crew was commander Jim Lovell, command module pilot Jack
    Swigert, and lunar module pilot Fred Haise.


Q5. How was the Lunar Module used during Apollo 13's return to Earth?
Expected response:
    After the Service Module was disabled, the Lunar Module (LM) served as a
    lifeboat: it provided life support (oxygen and power) for the crew and its
    engine was used for course-correction burns to guide the spacecraft back to
    Earth, since the Command Module's normal systems had to be powered down.


Q6. When did Apollo 11 land on the Moon?
Expected response:
    Apollo 11 landed on the Moon on July 20, 1969, when the Lunar Module "Eagle"
    touched down in the Sea of Tranquility.
